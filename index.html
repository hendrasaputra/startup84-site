Cake shop opens in Greenwich Village

Prices were high and customers were few. Nothing much sold until the pastry chef turned up.

A cake shop lives or dies on its name. Get the reputation up and it sells several times what an average one does; let it slip and the regulars drift off. It's one of four kinds of shop you can open, alongside the café, the bakery and the cookie kiosk. Pay for the licence once and you can open that kind in any city you've entered.

Bakery bins 40 loaves

Bread doesn't keep. The bakery bakes first thing and throws out whatever's left at closing time. Bake too little and you sell out by noon. Bake too much and it goes in the bin.

Two machines down at Blok M

Machines wear out with use and break down at random, more often when they're old. The manager had them repaired by closing time. The bill came out of the day's takings.

Cookie kiosk moves onto campus

A kiosk doesn't need a shopfront, just a pitch on the pavement. The rent is low and the cookies are cheap, but it can only sell so many.

Rival opens on Wall Street

Leave a street empty for long and a competitor will move in. If you want the spot, open there first.

Quiet week on campus

Students went home for the holidays and sales on campus halved. The manager asked to let six people go until term starts. The owner said no and kept them on.

Owner says yes to 13 machines

Managers decide by profit and loss. Anything big, like a layoff, a sale or a loan, waits for the owner. Leave it two days and the manager's choice goes ahead.
